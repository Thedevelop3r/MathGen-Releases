# MathGen privacy policy

MathGen does not collect personal data, and nothing you do in MathGen is
sent to its publisher.

- It has no accounts, analytics, advertising or crash reporting.
- Your equations, boards and settings stay on your computer: settings are
  saved in your Windows user profile (or next to the program in portable
  mode), and your work only where you save, copy, export or print it.
- Your name and MathGen ID are made and kept on your computer. The ID is
  random; it is not linked to you, your computer or any account.

## Live lessons

MathGen connects to other computers only when you use a Live lesson
(the Live tab):

- A teacher who starts a session shares their MathGen window (what it
  draws, and the mouse pointer) with the students who join with its code.
  Only MathGen is shared, never the rest of the screen.
- A student who joins sends their name and MathGen ID to the teacher's
  MathGen, so the teacher can see who is there and let them in. Answers to
  quick polls and raised hands go to the teacher too. A student can take a
  copy of the teacher's equation and board if the teacher allows it.
- The computers connect to each other directly, encrypted. When a direct
  connection isn't possible, the encrypted traffic passes through a relay
  server (the public relays of the networking library MathGen uses, or a
  school's own relay); the relay can't read it and nothing is stored.
- A teacher can save the session's list of names and IDs as a class file,
  on their own computer.

When the session ends, nothing about it is kept anywhere except what the
teacher or students saved themselves.

## The exam edition

The exam edition has no Live lessons and no network code at all; when it
is installed, Windows Firewall blocks it in both directions. It can put
the student's name and ID under exported pictures, if the school chose
that.
